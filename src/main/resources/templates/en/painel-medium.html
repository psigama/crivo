Each reading of yours was compared with the answers of the person who knew the person of that name and, for comparison, with the answers about the other person in the same request. What counts is the difference between the two counts. A single reading says little: chance explains many coincidences, and the study looks at the whole. The sitters' answers stay with them.

Technical detail

The person for comparison is the decoy of the pair: the other request, with a similar profile. The difference for the request is the d of the pair: across the two readings, the matches with the target minus the matches with the decoy. The fields where one of the sitters marked "I don't know" stay out of the comparison. The study's primary analysis is the paired Wilcoxon test over the d of all the pairs, as in the preregistration.

How a reading works

  1. The request arrives

    Two first names, in a drawn order, and the deadline declared in the study. You do not know who made the request, and the two names have similar profiles.

  2. You answer for each name

    The multiple-choice questions that count in the comparison. For each one, an option or "Nothing came".

  3. The reading locks

    Before locking, you review it. On locking, the answers are sealed and change no more.

  4. Scores at the end

    When the study is completed, you see the score of each reading of yours.
