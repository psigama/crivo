Summary

In a few words.

  • Someone who has lost a person gives that person's first name and answers multiple-choice questions about them.
  • A medium drawn at random receives only the first name, without knowing who asked, and answers the same questions.
  • The system compares those answers with the answers about the right person and with the answers about another, similar person, to measure how much chance explains.
  • Nobody sees a score before the end, and the rules were sealed before the start.
  • The result belongs to the whole study, and it is published, for or against.

How it works

The automated structured reading: eight steps, each one tied to the one before

Crivo's AIR protocol runs entirely through the site. The sitter and the medium answer the same closed questionnaire about the deceased person — the sitter because they knew them, the medium knowing only the first name. Two requests with a similar profile form a pair, and each one serves as the decoy for the other. Only what fits the true target better than it fits the decoy, consistently and above chance, counts as evidence.

  1. Preregistration

    The researcher declares hypotheses, inclusion criteria for mediums, the questionnaire, the number of pairs and the deadline — and the document is frozen with a hash before the first pair.

    the statistical power of the design is declared in the document itself

  2. Account and consent

    Each person creates an account with a fixed role — sitter or medium, never both — and confirms their e-mail. Identifiable data is kept encrypted, in a separate database schema.

  3. Locked request

    The sitter gives the first name and answers the questionnaire. The answers lock right away, and the trail receives a salted commitment to them, before any medium sees the request.

    commitment → revelation: the answers cannot change afterwards

  4. Pairing and draw

    Requests of the same gender, age band and period form pairs, and the medium for each pair is drawn from a seed whose commitment was published in the preregistration.

  5. Reading

    The medium receives only the two first names of the pair, in a drawn order, and answers the questionnaire for each one. On submission, the reading locks with a hash on the trail.

  6. Automatic unblinding

    When the declared number of pairs is complete, the system itself closes collection and reveals the seed and the salts. Nobody chooses the moment — not even the administrator.

  7. Scoring and analysis

    The answers are compared field by field, against the target and against the decoy, by a deterministic, published rule. The preregistered analysis runs, and the report comes out in the same format for any result.

    no score exists before unblinding, not even in the database

  8. Feedback

    Each sitter sees the medium's answers for their own target alongside their own answers; each medium sees the counts for their own readings. Everything leaves a trace on the hash-chained trail.

Reference only

This page describes a design that Crivo does not implement: the paired reading depends on a team — an experimenter and a redactor — and takes no participants. It stays here as a reference, because it is the protocol closest to the literature and it is against it that what the automated version gains and loses is measured.
