What this experiment is not. It is not a letter, nor a message sent by the person who died. It is also not a space for the sitter to ask questions and receive answers: the sitter asks the medium nothing, and the medium answers nobody's questions.

It is only an experiment to confirm — or not — the phenomenon of anomalous information reception. The medium, knowing only the first name, answers a closed questionnaire about the person, and the system checks whether those answers match the sitter's more than they match those of another person with a similar profile. The result concerns the study as a whole, not any one person.

Automated structured reading · P1

How to take part, step by step

Everything happens through the site. Nobody on the team reads what you write, and nobody judges whether an answer "fits": the system compares closed answers.

  1. Create an account

    At Create account, give your name, e-mail and a password, and accept the agreement. Your data is kept encrypted, separate from the research data, which uses only a code.

  2. Choose your role: sitter or medium

    A sitter is someone who lost a person and makes the request for a reading. A medium is someone who receives the requests and answers. The choice is made once and does not change: nobody can be on both sides.

  3. Confirm your e-mail

    You receive a confirmation link, valid for 48 hours. The account only becomes valid after it.

  4. Sitter: make the request

    Give the first name of the person who died and answer a multiple-choice questionnaire about them. On confirmation, the answers lock, and the public trail receives a cryptographic commitment to them before any medium sees the request.

  5. Medium: answer the request

    The system draws the medium and sends only the first name. The medium answers the same questionnaire — or marks "Nothing came" — and, on submission, the reading locks as well.

  6. Automatic checking and feedback

    When the study reaches the declared number of pairs, the system compares each reading with the true target and with a decoy of a similar profile. Only then do the scores appear: the sitter sees the medium's answers alongside their own, and the administrator receives the notice that the study has been completed.

The screens for each role

What each role sees

Each role sees only what their part allows: blinding is applied in the server and in the database, not by hiding fields on the screen. No score exists before the end of the study, for anyone.

Sitter

  • Dashboard — the state of your request, step by step.
  • New request — first name and questionnaire, with a review before locking.
  • Feedback — after the end of the study, the medium's answers alongside your own.
  • My account — your data, the agreement you accepted and deleting the account.

Medium

  • Dashboard — requests received and deadlines; readings submitted, with no score.
  • Reading — the first names of the pair and the blank questionnaire.
  • My account — your data, the agreement you accepted and deleting the account.

Administrator

  • Dashboard — accounts by role, requests and readings by step, progress towards the declared number: counts, never scores.
  • Notices — the completion of each step, from sign-up to the report.
  • Flags — accounts that look linked, for human review.
  • Study — preregistration, questionnaire, freezing and report.

Administrator is not a role anyone chooses: that account comes from the server configuration.

An honest state. Already working are account creation with a role, e-mail confirmation, the dashboard for each role, the request for a reading locked with a published commitment, the P1 study frozen by the administrator and the path up to the reading — pairing, the draw of the medium, sending with a deadline and resending, the locked reading — and the end of the study: at the declared number of pairs, the system itself unblinds, checks, analyzes, writes the report and releases the feedback, which becomes public at Studies and reports. Accounts that look like the same person are flagged for review, and a declared cap limits the pairs of each medium. The platform has not yet been used with real participants.
