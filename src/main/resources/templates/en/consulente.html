For anyone about to make a request

You have lost someone and want to take part. What happens.

This page is for you, who knew the person who died and are going to take part in the automated structured reading as a sitter. It explains what will be asked of you, why each requirement exists and what you can expect at the end.

First of all: you do not have to believe anything to take part, and nothing that happens here will be presented to you as a message. The study is about information and about agreement between answers; the meaning, if there is any, is yours and stays with you.

Taking part may stir things up in you. If at any point you want to stop, you only have to delete your account — with no notice of why, no justification and no penalty of any kind. This possibility is not a courtesy: it is part of the agreement you accept.

Step by step

What will happen, in order

  1. Account and consent

    You create an account with the sitter role and confirm your e-mail. The agreement says which data are kept and for how long. Data about religious conviction, about health and about the person who died are treated as sensitive by Brazilian law, and the consent for them is separate and set apart.

    the role is fixed: a sitter cannot be a medium, in any study

  2. You make the request

    You give the first name of the person who died and answer a multiple-choice questionnaire about them: how the death happened, where they lived, their temperament, some habits. Gender, age range at death and how long ago it happened are required, because they are what forms the pair. Before you send it, you review everything.

  3. Once sent, it locks

    Your answers can no longer change. At that same moment, the study's public record receives a digital seal of them — a kind of fingerprint, which does not show the answers — before any medium receives the request.

    this protects the study from an adjustment after the fact, and protects you from the suspicion of having adjusted anything

  4. Your request forms a pair

    The system puts your request together with that of another person, with a similar profile. Each request serves as the comparison for the other. If there is no compatible request yet, yours waits, and the dashboard says so.

  5. A medium drawn by lot answers without knowing you

    The medium receives only the two first names of the pair, in an order drawn by lot, and answers the same questionnaire for each of them. You do not speak with the medium, you do not know who they are, and they know nothing about you and do not see your answers. Nobody on the team sees any of this: the system does it all.

  6. Nobody sees a score before the end

    While the study is collecting, no score exists at all — not for you, not for the medium, not for the administrator. When the declared number of pairs is complete, the system itself closes the collection and does the scoring.

  7. Feedback

    You see the medium's answers for your person next to yours, field by field; how many agreed with yours and, for comparison, how many agreed with those of the other sitter in the pair — only the count, never their answers —; and what the study found overall.

How to answer

What is asked of you in the questionnaire

This is the part where the study depends on your honesty. There is no right answer, and there is no way for you to "help": a guessed answer does not improve the result, it only makes it less useful.

"I don't know" is a legitimate answer, and it is better than a guess. A field where you marked "I don't know" leaves the comparison for the whole pair — for your person and for the person for comparison — so that nobody gains an advantage. A wrong guess, on the contrary, counts as if it were information.

Answer from what you know

Think of the person you knew, and not of what they could have been. If two descriptions seem possible, choose the one that describes them best over most of their life.

Do not arrange anything with anyone

Do not tell mediums, or anyone who knows mediums, the first name you gave or that you made a request. The study is only worth something if the medium has no way of knowing whose each name is by another route.

One request at a time

Each sitter has one active request at a time. Several requests from the same person would make the observations depend on one another, and the study's analysis would no longer hold.

Your rights

What is guaranteed to you

Leave whenever you want

At any time, with no justification: deleting your account withdraws your request and erases the identifiable data. The record of your leaving keeps only a code and does not show the reason.

Your name stays out

In the research data you are a code. Your name, your e-mail, the first name of the person who died and your answers are kept encrypted, in a separate part of the database that only one part of the system can read.

Nothing personal in what is published

The public page of the studies shows the preregistration, counts and, at the end, the report and the verification package, with pseudonymous codes. The package carries the answers and the readings, so that anyone can redo the scoring — but never a name: neither yours, nor your e-mail, nor the first name of the person who died.

No machine judges

The scoring is a comparison of equality between options, by a published rule that anyone can redo. No artificial intelligence model reads or scores anything.

Feedback at the end

You receive an e-mail when it is available and see what the study found — including if it found nothing.

Nothing is said in your name

The platform does not present content as a message from a spirit, does not interpret anything from the point of view of any doctrine and does not publicize your taking part.

Honesty about the result

What this will not give you

The result belongs to the study, not to you and not to the person you lost. It is computed over dozens of pairs, and your pair on its own says nothing: common answers, such as "died of a heart condition", agree with a great many people, and chance explains a good part of the agreements in any individual reading.

A positive result overall also does not decide where the information came from: the answers exist in your memory and in the database, and the design does not distinguish between the possible explanations. And the most likely result, by what the literature shows to this day, is modest or null.

And now
