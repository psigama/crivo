Agreement

In six points

  1. What we ask for: your name and your e-mail. If you are a sitter, also information about the person who died, which Brazilian data protection law (LGPD) treats as sensitive data.
  2. What we store: your name and your e-mail are stored encrypted, separate from the research data, which use only a code.
  3. Who sees it: no one on the team reads your answers before the end of the study, and no artificial intelligence scores anything.
  4. What becomes public: at the end, the answers and the readings, with codes only — without your name, without your e-mail and without the first name of the person who died. Once published, they do not change, not even if you leave.
  5. How to leave: delete the account whenever you want, without giving a reason. The data that identify you are erased, and only the code remains.
  6. Who to talk to: a person answers at the contact address just below this form.

Full text

Your registration data — name and e-mail — are encrypted, in a database schema separate from the research data, which use only a code. Information about the deceased person is treated as sensitive data (LGPD). You can delete the account at any time, without giving a reason: the identifiable data are erased and only the code remains. No one on the team reads your answers before the end of the study, and there is no artificial intelligence in the scoring. At the end of the study, the verification package makes the questionnaire answers and the readings public, with codes only — without your name, without your e-mail and without the first name of the deceased person —, so that anyone can redo the check. Once published, the package does not change, not even if you delete the account.

Only people aged 18 or over take part. We do not ask for your date of birth.
