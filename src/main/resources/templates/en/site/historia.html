Timeline

One hundred and forty years of attempts

Nothing Crivo does is a new idea. What is new is enforcing in software what, until now, depended on the discipline of each team.

This timeline has a common thread: every advance came out of a criticism. The controls that look obvious today were, almost all of them, answers to a study that did not survive scrutiny — sometimes from the field itself, sometimes from a skeptic, sometimes from a professional magician.

1882 — 1930

The societies and the problem of control

  1. 1882

    Society for Psychical Research, London

    Founded by Cambridge academics — among them Henry Sidgwick and Frederic Myers — and by the physicist William Barrett. It is the first organization to investigate the subject systematically, with published reports.

  2. 1885

    American Society for Psychical Research

    Created in the United States with the participation of William James, of Harvard. James and Richard Hodgson studied the medium Leonora Piper for decades, trying to rule out fraud and telepathy.

  3. 1874 — 1920

    The problem that runs through all of it

    William Crookes publishes his sessions in 1874; Charles Richet coins the term “metapsychics”. That work is criticized today for insufficient controls, and several celebrated cases of the period were exposed as fraud.

    The lesson the field took a century to absorb: good faith is not a control.

  4. 1901 — 1932

    Cross-correspondences

    SPR investigators analyze fragmentary messages obtained through different mediums, which would only make sense put together. Some read a guiding intelligence into them; critics disputed that interpretation.

  5. 1930s

    J. B. Rhine and the Duke laboratory

    The study of psychic phenomena enters the mainstream university, with controlled experiments and statistical analysis. The question stops being “did it happen?” and becomes “above chance?”.

1967 — 2000

Institutionalization, and a blow from outside

  1. 1967

    Division of Perceptual Studies, University of Virginia

    Founded by the psychiatrist Ian Stevenson, it is still active. It studies reports of past-life memories, near-death experiences and mediumship inside a school of medicine.

  2. 1979 — 1983

    Project Alpha

    The illusionist James Randi placed two young magicians as alleged psychics in a university laboratory. They passed the tests. The episode did not prove that the phenomenon does not exist — it proved that a protocol designed without an adversary does not survive an adversary.

    That is why, here, every blinding rule has an automated test that tries to violate it, and the invitation to skeptics is part of the design, not a courtesy.

2001 — 2008

VERITAS: the modern phase and the criticism that reformed it

  1. 2001 — 2008

    VERITAS program, University of Arizona

    Directed by the psychologist Gary Schwartz, it tested mediums of wide visibility on American television, with blind evaluation, video recording and statistical scoring. It reported hits above chance level, and the book The Afterlife Experiments (2002) brought the subject to a broad audience.

  2. 2001 — 2003

    The methodological criticism

    Ray Hyman and Richard Wiseman pointed out insufficient controls against cold reading and sensory leakage, the absence of a prior stopping rule, scoring open to bias and a lack of standardization in the evaluation criteria.

    Three of the locks in Crivo come from here: a binding stopping rule, a scale frozen before collection and automatic refusal of the “confirmatory” label outside the declared sample.

  3. 2005

    O'Keeffe and Wiseman: the most useful null result in the field

    Five professional mediums, five sitters, acoustic isolation, blind judging and permutation analysis. A null result — and, with it, the most complete inventory of artifacts ever published, including one nobody had controlled for: the temporal cues inside the reading.

2007 — 2015

The blind protocols that Crivo inherits

  1. 2007

    Triple-blind protocol

    Beischel and Schwartz publish the design that becomes the reference: absent sitter, blind proxy experimenter, deceased persons paired by gender, decoy reading, transcription into items, global score and forced choice.

  2. 2008

    Windbridge, and quintuple blinding

    Julie Beischel, former research director of VERITAS, founds Windbridge to answer the criticisms with tighter controls. The protocol isolates five parties:

    1. the medium, who receives only the first name of the deceased;
    2. the sitter, who receives two coded transcripts and does not know which one is theirs;
    3. the experimenter who recruits and forms the pairs, with no contact with mediums;
    4. the experimenter who runs the session as a proxy, knowing only the first name;
    5. the experimenter who formats and distributes the transcripts, without knowing which is whose.

    The center also created an eight-step certification for research mediums, which includes psychological testing, two blind readings with significant performance, training in research ethics and training on grief. Screening is now closed: their own page says that “funding for the WCRM program has ended” and that they are no longer screening mediums.

  3. 2015

    Replication and extension

    Twenty certified mediums, 86 readings, five levels of blinding. The target readings were rated as more accurate than the decoys on several measures — in the section of objective questions, 52.8% against 36.6% item accuracy. The authors record the limitation: all the data come from a single laboratory.

    This is, literally, the reason Crivo exists: to make independent replication feasible and auditable.

2008 — 2022

Brazil, open science and taking stock

  1. 2008 and 2012

    Brazilian research on who the medium is

    Moreira-Almeida, Lotufo Neto and Cardeña compare Spiritist mediums with dissociative conditions and find them, in general, well adjusted and socially functional. Peres, Moreira-Almeida, Newberg and colleagues examine brain activity during mediumistic writing with SPECT. NUPES, at UFJF, establishes itself as a reference.

  2. 2014

    Chico Xavier's letters under a scale

    Rocha and colleagues assess 99 verifiable items in thirteen mediumistic letters, with separate scales for fit and for leakage — the latter measuring how plausible it would be to obtain that information by ordinary means.

  3. 2018

    The preregistration revolution

    Nosek and colleagues formalize, for science in general, the distinction between confirmatory and exploratory and the idea that deviations reported transparently are worth more than the absence of preregistration. It is the basis of the central lock of Crivo.

  4. 2021 — 2022

    Taking stock

    The meta-analysis by Sarraf, Woodley of Menie and Tressoldi estimates the aggregate effect at 6 to 14 points above chance level, with caveats about heterogeneity and publication bias. Silva and Moreira-Almeida compare studies with conflicting results and isolate what separates them. The prevailing scientific consensus remains that there is no reliable evidence.

Now

Where Crivo places itself

In 140 years, the field has learned what needs to be controlled. What never existed was a way to prove, afterwards, that it was controlled — and that is all this platform adds. Here the researcher is blinded as well, and the scores do not even exist before the unblinding; the preregistration is frozen with a hash before the first datum; the draw can be replayed by anyone from the revealed seed; and the event trail is refused by the database if anyone tries to alter it.

What this timeline does not cover

Other neighboring lines of research — near-death experiences, electronic communication devices, initiatives funding trials on the survival of consciousness — were left out on purpose. They are not studies of information accuracy under blinding, which is the scope of this platform, and we could not describe them here on the same documentary basis.
