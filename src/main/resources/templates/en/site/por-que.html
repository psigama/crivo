Why Crivo exists

Reports of mediums who get things right are not what is missing. What is missing is a method that holds up under doubt — and what makes that method hard is logistics, record-keeping and cryptography: a software problem.

First of all

What research on mediumship is, exactly

In the scientific literature, mediumship is described as the phenomenon in which someone — the medium — reports information about a deceased person to someone who knew them in life, called here the sitter — consulente, in the Portuguese of the project. That episode is the reading. The question research asks is narrower than the subject suggests, and it is precisely that narrowness that makes it answerable.

The question is: does the medium report accurate and specific information about the deceased person with no prior knowledge, with no sensory feedback from the person evaluating, and with no deceptive means?

That is all an experiment measures. So that the word itself does not already answer the question, the field calls the phenomenon anomalous information reception — AIR, for short. “Anomalous” here means only with no known explanation, not supernatural. The term describes what was observed — someone spoke, and what was said checks out — without asserting where the information came from.

Not a new field

Systematic investigation began in the nineteenth century: William Crookes published his sessions in 1874, and the Society for Psychical Research, founded in London in 1882, studied the medium Leonora Piper for decades, with Richard Hodgson and the psychologist William James among the researchers. That work is criticized today for the weakness of its controls, and several celebrated cases of the period were exposed as fraud. The modern phase is far more recent: blind, quantitative protocols only appeared from the end of the 1990s on, and levels of blinding have been rising ever since, largely as a direct response to skeptical criticism. The full timeline is here, and it is worth reading for one reason: every control that looks obvious today came out of a study that did not hold up under criticism.

Two branches that are often confused

One part of the research studies who the medium is: psychological profile, phenomenology of the experience, neuroimaging during trance. Brazilian work carries weight here — the comparison between Spiritist mediums and dissociative conditions, which found them generally well adjusted and socially functional, and the SPECT scans during psychography. That branch does not ask whether the information checks out.

The other part studies the accuracy of the information reported, under conditions that rule out the ordinary channels. Crivo deals only with this second branch. A blind protocol says nothing about anyone's mental health, and a neuroimaging scan says nothing about the deceased person.

What has to be ruled out

A hit is only of interest if the ordinary channels are closed. The literature names three, and a serious protocol is, in large part, the set of measures against them:

Cold reading
Inferring things about the deceased person from the appearance, clothing, behavior and reactions of the person sitting there — plus generic statements that fit almost everybody.
Warm reading
Obtaining information about the life of the person evaluating — an earlier conversation, social media — and deducing things about the deceased person from it.
Hot reading
Arriving at the session already knowing: prior research, obituaries, someone who told you beforehand.

To these are added two problems on the side of the person judging: evaluator bias, which tends to find matches when the person knows the reading is theirs, and after-the-fact selection of hits, which turns any long reading into proof. That is why the standard design hands over two unlabeled readings and asks for a choice: a generic statement fits both equally well.

What a result can and cannot say

Even a result above chance does not settle where the information came from. The authors of the favorable studies themselves record that the design does not distinguish survival of consciousness from other hypotheses, such as so-called super-psi. And the prevailing scientific consensus remains that there is no reliable evidence of the phenomenon — in good measure because of methodological flaws pointed out in the existing studies.

Where Crivo comes in

Crivo does not produce evidence and does not evaluate readings: it is the tool that makes those conditions executable and checkable. Controlling who knows what and when, randomizing with no way to cheat, freezing the analysis before the data, and proving afterwards that the order of events was indeed that — that is logistics, record-keeping and cryptography. It is a software problem, and it is the problem this project solves.

The problem

What is missing is not reports. It is a method that holds up under doubt.

Reports of mediums who get information about deceased people right are numerous, and they are almost never produced under conditions that rule out the conventional explanations: visual and verbal cues, cold reading, prior research on the participant, bias on the part of the person evaluating, fraud, and after-the-fact selection of hits. The literature shows that this can be studied rigorously — what makes these protocols hard is logistics and discipline: controlling who knows what, when, and proving it afterwards. That is a software problem.

Blinding depends on people's good will and memory.

Blinding is enforced by the system: each role sees only what it is allowed to see, in the data layer, not just on the screen.

Draws done by hand, with no record.

A draw by cryptographic generator, with the commitment published beforehand and the seed revealed at the end.

Hypotheses and analysis adjusted after seeing the data.

Preregistration frozen and hashed before the first session. An amendment creates a new version; the previous one stays visible.

No way to prove the order of events.

An event trail chained by hash, which the database refuses to alter or delete.

Null results do not get published.

The same report, with the same prominence, whatever the result.

Who it is for

Two audiences that tend to avoid each other

The project only makes sense if it serves both at the same time. The rigor that convinces one is what gives the other's result its value.

Researchers, skeptics and committees

What you get:

  • A preregistration with a hash and a timestamp, predating the first datum.
  • A draw reproducible on your own machine, from the revealed seed.
  • An event trail the database prevents from being altered, verifiable from the command line.
  • An exact, deterministic analysis, validated against reference values.
  • A hashed report, published under Studies and reports, and each study's verification package, without names, which you check on your own machine with one command, with no access to the server.

Spiritist groups, mediums and families

What the protocol protects:

  • Neutral vocabulary: reading, item, target. Nothing is presented as a message from a spirit.
  • Privacy by design: sensitive data encrypted, pseudonymization, Brazilian data protection law (LGPD).
  • Withdrawal at any time, with no need to give a reason.
  • Care for bereaved people, from the language to the feedback at the end of the study.
  • A result nobody will be able to dismiss by saying the method was loose.
