Bibliographic references

What this rests on

Every rule of the protocol comes from somewhere. Here are the works that support it — including those that concluded the opposite.

The list deliberately includes the critics and the null results. A protocol built only on the favorable literature would not survive the first careful reading, and a good part of the locks in this system came precisely from the objections below.

1 · Blinding protocols

The basis of the paired protocol

  1. Beischel, J., & Schwartz, G. E. (2007). Anomalous information reception by research mediums demonstrated using a novel triple-blind protocol. Explore, 3(1), 23–27. doi:10.1016/j.explore.2006.10.004

    Origin of the v1 structure: blind proxy experimenter, same-gender pairs, decoy reading, transcription into items, global score and forced choice. It is also the source of the observation that forced choice is the least sensitive measure — which led Crivo to adopt the paired difference as its primary measure.

  2. Beischel, J., Boccuzzi, M., Biuso, M., & Rock, A. J. (2015). Anomalous information reception by research mediums under blinded conditions II: Replication and extension. Explore, 11(2), 136–142. doi:10.1016/j.explore.2015.01.001

    Grounds the “first name only” rule, the remote sessions and the three measures of the analysis. The effect sizes by reading section (d from 0.49 to 0.75) are the basis of the power calculation. The authors themselves record the limitation: all the data come from a single laboratory — which is, to a large extent, the reason this platform exists.

2 · Criticism and null results

The works that changed the design most

  1. O'Keeffe, C., & Wiseman, R. (2005). Testing alleged mediumship: Methods and results. British Journal of Psychology, 96(2), 165–179. doi:10.1348/000712605X36361

    A null result, and the most complete inventory of artifacts: sensory leakage, the generality of the statements and the need for blind evaluation. Four of the locks in Crivo come from here — one item equal to one statement, the randomized order of the items, the counterbalancing of session times against temporal cues and the balance of length between the two readings of a pair.

  2. Kelly, E. W., & Arcangel, D. (2011). An investigation of mediums who claim to give information about deceased persons. The Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease, 199(1), 11–17. doi:10.1097/NMD.0b013e31820439da

    The blinding was considered incomplete because the mediums received photographs of the deceased. In Crivo, the blinding matrix treats any information beyond the first name as leakage.

  3. Hyman, R. (2003). How not to test mediums: Critiquing the afterlife experiments. Skeptical Inquirer, 27(1), 20–30.

    The criticism that weighs most on the current design: the absence of a prior stopping rule, scoring open to bias and the manner of scoring itself as a source of spurious results. It is answered by the binding stopping rule, the scale frozen before collection and the automatic refusal of the “confirmatory” label outside the declared sample.

  4. Simmons, J. P., Nelson, L. D., & Simonsohn, U. (2011). False-positive psychology. Psychological Science, 22(11), 1359–1366. doi:10.1177/0956797611417632

    Shows how researcher degrees of freedom produce positive results out of noise. It is the general argument behind freezing the analysis, the sample and the criteria before the first datum.

3 · Reviews and meta-analyses

The effect size one can expect

  1. Sarraf, M., Woodley of Menie, M. A., & Tressoldi, P. (2021). Anomalous information reception by mediums: A meta-analysis of the scientific evidence. Explore, 17(5), 396–402. doi:10.1016/j.explore.2020.04.002

    Estimates the aggregate effect at 6 to 14 percentage points above chance level. It is the number that sizes the study: with it, forced choice would require hundreds of pairs, and the paired measure requires dozens. It is built into the power calculation that the platform writes into the preregistration.

  2. Silva, J., & Moreira-Almeida, A. (2022). Controlled experiments involving anomalous information reception with mediums: An analysis of recent studies. Journal of Scientific Exploration, 36(4), 749–759. doi:10.31275/20222575

    Compares studies with conflicting results and isolates what separates them: screening of mediums, the motivation of the person who evaluates, free speech combined with objective questions, and not overloading the person who evaluates with too many items. Each of these points became a preregistrable field or a limit in the system.

  3. Milton, J., & Wiseman, R. (1999). Does psi exist? Lack of replication of an anomalous process of information transfer. Psychological Bulletin, 125(4), 387–391. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.4.387

    A reminder that meta-analysis does not replace independent replication. Hence the verification package, designed so that another group can rerun the analysis without depending on this one.

4 · Brazilian context

Research done here

  1. Rocha, A. C., Paraná, D., Freire, E. S., Lotufo Neto, F., & Moreira-Almeida, A. (2014). Investigating the fit and accuracy of alleged mediumistic writing: A case study of Chico Xavier's letters. Explore, 10(5), 300–308. doi:10.1016/j.explore.2014.06.002

    Brazilian case study on mediumistic writing, with separate scales for fit and for leakage per item. The idea of assessing, item by item, how plausible it would be to obtain that by ordinary means is an extension planned for Crivo. Mediumistic writing itself is outside the scope of v1, because it depends on documentary judgment that cannot be automated in a blind protocol.

5 · Open science

Preregistration and reproducibility

  1. Nosek, B. A., Ebersole, C. R., DeHaven, A. C., & Mellor, D. T. (2018). The preregistration revolution. PNAS, 115(11), 2600–2606. doi:10.1073/pnas.1708274114

    Theoretical basis for the distinction between confirmatory and exploratory, and for the idea that deviations reported transparently are worth more than the absence of preregistration. From here comes the record of protocol deviations as an entity of its own, which is never erased.

One analysis barred in this project. Multiplying the individual probabilities of the items as if they were independent, the method of Hyslop (1919), inflates accuracy and was refuted in the literature of the field itself. The alternative adopted is the permutation test of Pratt and Birge, which does not assume independence between items of the same reading.

The complete bibliography, with a summary of each work and the map of “criticism → control in the system”, is versioned in the repository, in docs/REFERENCES.md. Is a relevant reference missing, for or against? Open an issue.

6 · By the author

A book by the person who maintains Crivo

  1. Cisi, R. R. L. (2026). O sistema psíquico: uma abordagem baseada na psicologia analítica e em conceitos de espiritualidade (rev. ed.; 1st ed. 2021). São Paulo: RRLC. doi:10.5281/zenodo.24370051 · Amazon

    An essay that puts Jung's analytical psychology in dialogue with spiritualist traditions. It grounds no rule of the protocol, which rests only on the works above; it is here so that the origin of the author's interest in the subject is known.
