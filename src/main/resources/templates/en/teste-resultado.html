The derivation rule is this, and it is the whole rule:

seed = SHA-256(server half || your half)
target(n) = SHA-256(seed || "trial:" || n || counter) mod 4

This session predates participant entropy: the seed came entirely from the server. The automatic check here only applies to sessions run after that change; this one can be checked by hand, by the rule above, with the revealed seed.

How to know this is the same seed as at the start

That is the question that decides everything. Recomputing the targets from a seed is only worth something if the seed was already fixed before your answers — otherwise it would be enough to pick, at the end, a seed that made you miss.

The system published hash before you drew your half. It is the SHA-256 of: 

server half | protocol | trials | alternatives | figure pool

Hence the numbers do not work out for a dishonest server: to choose targets that made you miss, it would need a half that, combined with yours, gave the desired result — and it had already published the hash of its own half before seeing yours. That is a preimage attack on SHA-256.

The button above checks both things: that the revealed half matches the commitment, and that the two halves together give the seed that generated the targets. It also checks, where possible, against the commitment that your browser stored at the start of the session.

What this verification still does not prove

  • If the session began in another browser, in a private window, or with local storage blocked, the commitment from the start was not stored. Then the check of the order of events again depends on the platform's record — and anyone who wants the complete proof copies the commitment on the first screen and compares it at the end.
  • The order of events is recorded in the audit trail, chained by hash: the opening of the session, with the commitment, is entered before each answer. But the trail is kept by this same application — it proves internal consistency, not the honesty of whoever operates it.
  • The proof that does not depend on trusting us is someone else reimplementing the rule from outside. That is why it fits in eight lines, is written out here in full and has a public test vector: a seed of 64 zeros, 12 trials, 4 alternatives, targets 1, 1, 3, 3, 2, 3, 2, 1, 1, 4, 1, 3.

The reference implementation is in support/SeedStream.java and in trials/TargetDerivation.java. The commitment ties the seed to the session parameters: protocol, number of trials, number of alternatives and version of the figure pool.
